It's been an endlessly soft autumn in New York City, and a year since my last dispatch. Today I'm thrilled to release **Unda Maris**, an electronic audio device I've developed over the last year. You can read more about it on [Metalabel](https://ultramarine.metalabel.com/unda-maris), where I have copies for sale from the edition of 25.

![Unda Maris](https://reubenson.com/public/unda-maris/main.jpg){style="width:100%; max-width:660px; height:auto; display:block;"}

I won't say too much about it here, save for the fact that it's a work that's difficult to place its point of genesis exactly. Perhaps it began when I first learned about the Gateway Process and the CIA-funded research into the military applications of remote viewing. Or when I walked into a medieval church in Tours on a hot July afternoon four years ago and listened to an organist rehearsing a fragment of a song; the song still unknown to me but the breadth of its reverberations still lodged in my neurons. Or perhaps it was when I stepped into Alvin Lucier's classroom on my first day of college, a composer whose music I'm still metabolizing after all these years.

However it began, I'm excited to be sharing it after a year in development, and I'll be hosting a launch/listening party with [Ergot Records](https://www.instagram.com/ergotrecords/) on Thursday, December 3rd. I hope to see some of you there!

I'll also continue to sell non-editioned ceramic objects on my web shop, where I have a new section for [**incense trays**](https://reubenson.com/shop#incense-trays). These flared and floating forms perhaps echo Lucy Rie's bowls and the conical design of audio speakers (see also the Japanese tradition of monkō, "listening to incense"). I've also been experimenting with a variation on the same form, adding a snip of nichrome wire that works as a post for candles.

![Candlestick holder](https://reubenson.com/public/newsletter/incense-tray.jpg){style="width:100%; max-width:660px; height:auto; display:block;"}

Separately from the processes of object-making, I've been an ambivalent about the presentation of product images. I recently read Janet Malcolm's collection of memoir essays, [Still Photos](https://www.jewishbookcouncil.org/book/still-pictures-on-photography-and-memory), in which she describes her early career at _The New Yorker_ writing shopping guides **without** the accompaniment of images.

![Janet Malcolm Desk](https://reubenson.com/public/newsletter/janet-malcolm-desk.jpg){style="width:100%; max-width:660px; height:auto; display:block;"}
_Image generated using [Janet Malcolm's description](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/1966/09/10/about-the-house) of [a desk by Hans Wegner](https://www.1stdibs.com/furniture/tables/desks-writing-tables/hans-j-wegner-drop-leaf-desk-table-model-at-305-andreas-tuck-1955/id-f_31740572/)_ in 1966.{style="font-size:12px; color:#666;"}

The indexical function of the product image is relocated into the _latent space of content_ navigated by language itself, which can prompt an image from an LLM more consistently than into the mind of the reader. On Shopify's [evergreen page on developing MVPs](https://www.shopify.com/blog/minimum-viable-product), the early deployment of marketing materials is recommended as a means of validating the product's market before investment in production, a strategy enabled by the prevalance of dropshipping, industries of white-label production, and the normalization of crowdfunding.

In the migration of the product image from _proof of life_ to _proof of sale_, they become image-products: speculative objects freely floating in economies of goods and attention. As minor egregores of consumer desire, these images are free to exist without a destiny bound to material substrate, and only sometimes _dropshipped into reality_ once the weight of their stochastic gradient descent plunges them down into meatspace. In online "dopamine shops," the sustain-release cycle of dopamine is choreographed by simulating the shopping experience entirely. These images have an existence like medieval birds of paradise, which were thought to live in the clouds surviving on atmospheric dew alone, until death.

> "Thus, in adult life, the quest is not to possess the object; rather the object is pursued in order to surrender to it as a medium that alters the self" -- Christopher Bollas, _The Shadow of the Object: Psychoanalysis of the Unthought Known_ (1987).

That's it for now — see you next time!
